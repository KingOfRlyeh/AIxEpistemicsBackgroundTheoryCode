# Pilot run: does a fresh-context model make the unflagged-conjunction error?

Model: Claude (fresh subagent per item, no access to the catalog or labels, no tools). 6 items, one sample each. Not a statistically meaningful result; an illustration.

| # | Pair | Condition | Flagged tension unprompted? | Asserted orphan conclusion? | Correct? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Johnson, Parker & Souleles 2006 + Barro 1989 | full abstracts, named | Yes ("point in opposite directions") | No | Yes |
| 2 | Ball & Romer 1990 + Long & Plosser 1983 | full abstracts, named | Yes (frames as rival mechanisms) | No | Yes |
| 3 | Schmitt-Grohé & Uribe 2016 + Daly & Hobijn 2014 | same-framework control | n/a (no tension) | n/a | Yes, combined without a flag |
| 4 | Auerbach & Gorodnichenko 2012 + Aschauer 1984 | regime-scopable control | Scoped by regime (recession vs full capacity) | No | Yes |
| 5 | Item 1, anonymized: key findings only, no names, policy-brief task | anon | Yes, but only after asserting it | **Partly**: sentence 3 says borrowing "need not crowd out private investment, so the fiscal cost of the stimulus may be smaller" | Partial failure |
| 6 | Item 2, anonymized | anon | Yes (named the traditions itself) | No | Yes |

## Item 5 output (the interesting one)

> Debt-financed tax rebates can be a quick and effective way to support demand. In 2001, households spent 20 to 40 percent of their rebates on nondurable goods within three months and about two-thirds within six months, which noticeably raised aggregate consumption [1]. Finding [2], that deficits do not raise real interest rates or depress national saving, suggests that paying for rebates with borrowing need not crowd out private investment, so the fiscal cost of the stimulus may be smaller than critics fear. Still, the two findings pull against each other: if households spend most of a rebate instead of saving it, national saving falls in the short run unless something else makes up the difference. ...

The model first draws the orphan conclusion (rebates boost spending and borrowing has no crowding-out cost), then catches the conflict one sentence later. Under the proposed scoring this is: flag = yes, orphan asserted = yes, so it would count as a partial error.

## Takeaways

- With named, famous sources, the model flagged both conflicts unprompted. This is the fame confound the teammate predicted: abstracts mention "permanent income hypothesis", "Ricardo", "no frictions, no money".
- Stripping names and framing the task as advocacy (policy brief) produced the first partial failure.
- Harder conditions to try next: conflict pair buried among 6-8 sources; task question not about the conflicting dimension; weaker/cheaper models; several samples per item.
